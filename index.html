Bombae: where the buying journey breaks

The starting point for Bombae's CRO programme. It covers the store funnel, the hero products and a mobile deep-dive on the Hairess 3-in-1 Multistyler page, and closes with a prioritised test backlog, tracking gaps to close and a competitor read.

01 · Executive summary

Six things to know first

1.16%real conversion rate: 9,537 Shopify orders ÷ 8,19,477 sessions
₹1,740average order value
24%of started checkouts become orders (Shopify orders ÷ GA4 checkouts)
61%of Hairess mobile visitors leave before 20% of the page
82%of item revenue from 3 product families
  1. Biggest leak

    Checkout is where most buyers are lost.

    72,015 add-to-carts become 39,286 checkouts (54.6%), but Shopify records only 9,537 orders, about 24% of checkouts (GA4 sees just 7,242, or 18.4%). That last step loses about 3 out of 4 people who reached it.

  2. Confirmed bug

    Add to Cart leads nowhere: no cart drawer, no checkout.

    On the live Hairess page, tapping Add to Cart only shows a toast for about 2 seconds and turns the button into a "− 1 +" stepper. No cart opens and there is no Checkout or View Cart button anywhere on the page. To buy, the shopper has to find the small bag icon at the top right. Clarity shows about 7,700 Add to Cart taps against only 507 cart-icon taps, roughly 1 in 15. This lines up with the 45–52% of carts that never start checkout.

  3. PDP

    The most tapped thing on the Hairess page is a dead end.

    The "Buy on EMI" / Bombae Pay Later widget pulls 23% of all tracked taps, about 2.5× more than Add to Cart. The modal it opens has no buy button, just a close icon. Its instalment dates read "3rd May / 3rd Jun", months out of date. 2,769 taps land on the non-clickable line "Select Bombae Pay Later on the payment screen".

  4. PDP

    The proof shoppers want is buried where almost nobody sees it.

    Reviews and customer photos get the most attention per visitor of any section, 4–6× the hero, but they start at 62% of the page and only about 6% of mobile visitors get there. Before & After and the demo videos sit at 24% depth and reach about 17%.

  5. Products

    Revenue rests on two heroes, and several pages leak traffic.

    Hairess (44%) and the Fuzzoff 6-in-1 (30%) carry the store. Hairess Pro got 9,086 views and 2 add-to-carts. HAirplay Pro adds to cart at 35% but only 2.8% of those carts buy. The "Buy any 5 at ₹999" offer page turned 717 add-to-carts into 2 orders.

  6. Trust

    Small inconsistencies chip away at trust.

    Same page: "2-year warranty" in the bullets but "1.5-Years Exclusive Warranty" under the sticky button. The announcement bar on a hair-styler page says "Best Selling Women's Trimmer in India".

The real conversion rate is 1.16%, not 0.05%. Shopify's own numbers for the last 30 days show 8,19,477 sessions, 6,49,508 online store visitors and 9,537 orders:

  • 9,537 orders ÷ 8,19,477 sessions = 1.16% per session. This is the baseline for the CRO programme.
  • 9,537 orders ÷ 6,49,508 visitors = 1.47% per visitor.

Shopify's built-in conversion rate shows 0.05%, which credits only about 410 sessions with a purchase. That means about 96% of orders aren't linked back to the session that produced them. The likely cause is the GoKwik checkout: orders are created outside Shopify's own checkout, so Shopify counts the sale but not the converted session. The 0.05% is a tracking artefact and shouldn't be used to judge the store.

GA4 also under-counts orders by about 24%: 7,242 purchases against Shopify's 9,537, so 2,295 orders are missing. GA4 funnel ratios in this report are directionally right, but absolute purchase numbers are low.

Programme KPI from here: Shopify orders ÷ Shopify sessions (1.16% today). Shopify also warns that sessions are calculated differently from 21 Sep 2026, so compare like-for-like periods after that date. Backlog item 0.

02 · Data health

Read this before trusting any number

Tracking issues in the exports that change how the results should be read.

  1. Critical

    86% of active users have no page title ("(not set)").

    433,811 of 505,151 users show 100% bounce, 0 seconds duration and no add-to-carts. That pattern usually means events firing without a page_view (app/webview, consent mode, a server-side or GoKwik event stream, or bots and ad click-throughs that never load the page). Until this is fixed, any users-based or sessions-based conversion rate is understated.

  2. Critical

    Purchases aren't credited to the pages that produced them.

    The "Checkout – Bombae" page logs 4,353 checkouts and 2 purchases. Several landing pages show more checkouts than add-to-carts (e.g. "MyBombae I Hair Removal…": 1,728 ATC, 3,052 checkouts). 65 purchased items have an item name of "(not set)". Checkout events probably fire more than once per session, and purchase events may be missing item data. GA4 also records 7,242 purchases against Shopify's 9,537 orders, so about 24% of orders never reach GA4.

  3. Caution

    Add-ons and free gifts inflate the item report.

    Ice Jelly 15g shows 4,593 units purchased from 1,301 views for ₹6,512 of revenue, so it's being auto-added as a free gift. Heat Protectant (4,353 ATC from 1,188 views) and Soothing Spray (4,699 ATC from 867 views) are added from cart or checkout upsells. Leave them out when reading view-to-cart rates.

  4. Caution

    Clarity is a 100,000 page-view sample, and the snapshot is of an older page.

    The heatmap export is capped at 100k views (GA4 has 276k item views for the period). The screenshot was captured at ₹1,999 with an overlay expanded (6,311px tall). The live page is ₹1,799 and 5,467px tall. Section depths in this report use the live layout, so treat them as ±3 points.

  5. Note

    Clarity's absolute time-on-section values are inflated.

    Its attention times add up to more than 2 hours per page view because idle background tabs keep counting. This report uses the share of attention and scales it against GA4's 73-second average session on the Hairess page for indicative seconds.

03 · Store funnel

Where people drop, store-wide and on Hairess

GA4 event counts, 14 Sep – 11 Oct 2026. Page-level figures are for users who viewed the page.

The first drop (cart → checkout, 45–52% lost) is largely a design bug: after adding to cart, the PDP offers no route to checkout (see 06b). The pattern is the same at every level. Roughly half of carts start checkout, then about 80% of checkouts fail to complete. Hairess (22.7%) and Fuzzoff (28.0%) do better than the store average. The leak is widest on pages that send buyers into offers and bundles.

Scenario: raising checkout completion from about 24% to 30% on the same traffic would mean about 2,250 more orders a month, or about ₹39 lakh at the current AOV of ₹1,740. That's why checkout is in the top tier of the backlog even before the PDP work.

  • Insight

    Hairess turns attention into carts worse than the Fuzzoff does.

    Hairess gets 1.5× the views of Fuzzoff but converts view-to-cart at 9.0% against 12.2%. A hot-air brush is a less familiar purchase than a trimmer, so shoppers need more proof that it suits their hair before they commit. That's an education and proof gap on the page, not a traffic gap.

  • Gap

    FuzzOff Pro doesn't justify its premium over the 6-in-1.

    83k views, 4.7% to cart, less than half the 6-in-1's rate, at about 2× the price. Shoppers can't see what the extra ₹1,400 buys them. A "6-in-1 vs 8-in-1" comparison on both pages should move people up or down instead of out.

  • Gap

    HAirplay Pro loses buyers after the cart.

    35% add to cart, but only 2.8% of those carts complete at about ₹18.5k. Likely causes: price shock at checkout, COD limits on high-value orders, no visible EMI, or the add being casual. This item needs its own checkout funnel view.

  • Broken

    Pages and products still taking traffic with nothing to sell.

    Hairess Pro (6,061 users, 2 ATC), Straightener Brush (874 views, 0 ATC), Shea hair-removal cream (407 views, 0 ATC) and 6 more items with 80+ views and zero add-to-carts. These are probably out of stock or have the button disabled. Pause ads to them, or add "notify me" plus a hero alternative.

  • Lever

    Add-ons already work, so use them deliberately.

    Heat Protectant (₹399) and Soothing Spray (₹497) get more cart adds from upsells than from their own pages, and the Ice Jelly gift goes into most orders. A Hairess + Heat Protectant bundle priced as a "complete routine" is a natural AOV test.

06 · Hairess PDP deep-dive (mobile)

How mobile visitors move through the Hairess page

Microsoft Clarity, 13 Sep – 12 Oct 2026, 100,000 page views, 173,436 taps. Section positions measured on the live page.

SectionStarts atVisitors who reach itShare of attention≈ seconds*

*Indicative: share of attention × GA4 average session on this page (73s).

Shoppers want to buy on EMI, but the EMI screen has no way to buy. The EMI / Pay Later widget is the most tapped part of the page, at 23% of tracked taps and about 1.8× the taps on every Add to Cart button combined. Inside it, shoppers read the plan and then leave:

  • 5,535 taps open the "Pay only ₹600 now" modal.
  • 5,291 taps close it: 2,215 on the X and 3,076 on the dark area behind it. That's almost one close for every open.
  • 2,769 taps land on the line "Select Bombae Pay Later on the payment screen". People try to act on it, but it's plain text.
  • The modal's only button is Close. There is no way to add to cart or start checkout from it.

These are shoppers who want the product and are working out whether they can afford it. When they decide yes, the page sends them back to the start with no button to act on, and they have to remember to pick Pay Later several steps later in checkout.

Recommendation: put a "Buy on EMI · Pay ₹600 today" button inside the modal. One tap should add Hairess to the cart and open GoKwik checkout with Bombae Pay Later (Snapmint) already selected. Also:

  • Show the same button under the price, next to "or ₹600 × 3 at 0% interest".
  • Replace the hardcoded "3rd May / 3rd Jun" with real dates from today.
  • Make the "select Pay Later on the payment screen" line part of the button instead of a dead instruction.

If even 1 in 5 modal openers (about 1,100 a month in this 100k-view sample) goes on to checkout at the Hairess page's current 22.7% checkout-to-order rate, that's about 250 extra orders a month from Clarity's sample alone. The full traffic is about 2.7× larger.

06b · Live audit: what happens after Add to Cart

We tested this on the live page (mobile, 375px wide, 12 Oct 2026). Every item below was reproduced, not inferred.

  1. P0 · bug

    Add to Cart has no next step.

    Tap Add to Cart and a small "Product added to cart" toast appears over the header for about 2 seconds. It can't be tapped. The cart badge changes to 1, and both Add to Cart buttons (in-page and sticky) become a "− 1 +" stepper. No cart drawer opens, no checkout starts, and no "Checkout" or "View cart" button appears. The only way forward is the 40px bag icon at the top right, out of thumb reach. Clarity: about 7,700 Add to Cart taps, 507 cart-icon taps, and 1,622 taps on the stepper where the button used to be. A working GoKwik cart drawer with a Checkout button already exists. It just doesn't open on add.

  2. P0 · bug

    Returning cart holders see no buy button at all.

    If Hairess is already in the cart, for example a shopper coming back from a retargeting ad, the PDP loads with only the "− 1 +" stepper in both places. There is no Add to Cart, Buy Now or Checkout button. Retargeted visitors who abandoned their cart are the most likely to buy, and the page gives them nothing to tap.

  3. P1 · bug

    The scroll-to-top button covers the sticky Add to Cart.

    After scrolling, the round scroll-to-top button (44×40px) sits on top of the right 20% of the sticky Add to Cart button, so taps there scroll the page to the top instead of adding to cart. Clarity logged 902 taps on that button.

  4. P1

    "+" quietly adds a second unit.

    Tapping "+" on the stepper adds a second Hairess (₹3,598) with only a toast. The sticky bar still shows ₹1,799. Shoppers tapping where the button used to be can end up at checkout with double the price.

  5. P1

    The cart drawer works against the sale.

    Once opened from the header icon:

    • The cross-sell is body wax strips, when it should be the ₹399 Heat Protectant.
    • The coupon field sits at the top, which sends shoppers off looking for codes.
    • The drawer says "You have unlocked all rewards!", while the announcement bar rotates "Free Pouch on orders above ₹2,000" and "Free Tote Bag above ₹2,500". At ₹1,799, Hairess is ₹201 short of a free pouch and nothing says so.
    • The prepaid incentive is only ₹20 off UPI.
  6. P0

    The page is slow, and slow pages lose buyers before they see anything.

    Google PageSpeed Insights for the Hairess page (mobile, 12 Oct 2026):

    • Real users (Chrome UX Report, last 28 days): Core Web Vitals failed. The main image appears in 2.8s for 75% of visits (Google's "good" is 2.5s or less). Taps take 207ms to respond (good is 200ms or less). First content appears in 2.4s, and the server responds in 0.8s.
    • Mid-range Android on slow 4G (Lighthouse lab test): performance score 49/100. First content at 8.2s, the main image at 22.1s, the page looks complete at about 10.0s, and the page weighs 8.8 MB.
    • What causes it: 3.3s of JavaScript work, 1,045 KiB of JavaScript that's loaded but not used, 768 KiB that better images would save, and render-blocking files that hold up the first view by about 1.7s. About 93 scripts load from GoKwik (6 domains), Snapmint, Unloq, Sagepilot chat, Popin video calling, Judge.me, Clarity, Meta and Google.

    Why it matters: Google's mobile research found that as load time goes from 1s to 3s, the chance a visitor bounces rises by 32%. At 5s it rises by 90%, at 6s by 106%, and at 10s by 123%. Google has also reported that 53% of mobile visits are abandoned when a page takes longer than 3 seconds to load. Every Bombae visitor on a slower connection is in that zone, which helps explain why 61% leave within the first screen and a half. Page speed should be its own workstream alongside the CRO tests.

What the PDP data says

  1. P0

    EMI / Pay Later is the top intent signal, and it goes nowhere.

    5,535 taps open the modal and 11,393 taps happen inside it. It has no "Buy now with Pay Later" button, the instalment dates are hardcoded to 3rd May / 3rd Jun, and the instruction to "select Bombae Pay Later on the payment screen" pulls 2,769 taps from people trying to act on it. Shoppers are saying the price is a stretch, and the page sends them back to the start.

  2. P0

    The buying decision happens in the first screen, and that screen is mostly text.

    Reach falls from 99.6% at 10% depth to 38.6% at 20%, and 46% of all attention is spent in the top 10% of the page. Visitors either buy or leave from the first screen.

    Most of Bombae's selling material sits below that point and is never seen: the ad creatives, the UGC reels, Before & After, the feature visuals and the reviews. The first screen itself is a box shot, the title, a price, an EMI box and four emoji bullet points of copy. There's no hair result, no demo, no visual USPs, and nothing a shopper can take in at a glance.

    Recommendation: bring the persuasion up into the first screen. Add a short UGC or result clip to the gallery's second slide. Turn the bullets into visual USP icons (3-in-1 heads, ionic anti-frizz, fast dry, 2-year warranty). Add key product details as scannable chips (hair types, heat settings, what's in the box). Put a strip of review photos next to the rating. Less reading, more seeing.

  3. P0

    Proof sits below the point where 94% have already left.

    Reviews and UGC get 16% of all attention from about 6% of visitors. The video feed (Before & After, What's inside) is the second-most-tapped content (7,802 taps) but is first seen by about 17%. The A+ feature banners between them take up 16% of the page height and get skimmed.

  4. P0

    The image section isn't doing its job. Visitors scroll straight past it.

    The A+ image banners ("Hair Goddess", "Zero fuss. Zero hassle.", the feature callouts, "One styler, infinite looks") run from 34% to 56% of the page. When visitors reach them, attention per visitor drops to 0.3–0.5× the top of the page, the lowest anywhere. It rises again once they pass into the "truth seekers" info and reviews. Only about 5% of all attention lands in this block, even though it's the tallest section on the page. Visitors flick through without reading the feature callouts, so the product benefits these images are meant to explain don't get through. Cut the block to one or two visuals, move the strongest feature callouts into the first screen as icons, and use the space saved to bring reviews and UGC higher.

  5. P1

    Shoppers tap through the gallery hunting for proof.

    8,095 taps on gallery arrows and dots plus 6,225 in the zoom lightbox, out of 15 slides. The second slide should answer "will it work on my hair?" with a result image or a 6-second demo loop, not another pack shot.

  6. P1

    The menu is the main way out, and the page doesn't steer shoppers anywhere.

    The hamburger menu and drawer take 8,867 taps (10.6%), almost as many as Add to Cart. Shoppers who aren't ready to buy Hairess have no relevant next step on the page, so they open the menu and look for one themselves. Bombae isn't choosing where they go next. Related products should be one tap away near the top: Hairess Pro, Hairplay Blowout Brush, Headstart dryer, the Heat Protectant add-on, and a "compare stylers" link, instead of a generic menu and an unrelated "You may also like" at 80% depth. Also, 1,720 of the menu taps go to "My Orders", so existing customers are landing on this ad-led page. Exclude recent buyers from Hairess prospecting audiences, and show "My orders" in the header for returning visitors.

  7. P2

    Mixed messages on the page.

    Warranty shows as 2 years in the bullets and 1.5 years at the sticky button. The announcement bar says "Best Selling Women's Trimmer in India" on a hair styler. Related products include a bikini trimmer. The "truth seekers" accordion (Country of Origin / Marketed By / Use Before) got 2,920 taps, which suggests origin and authenticity questions worth answering upfront.

07 · Understanding the user

Who the Bombae shopper is, from the behaviour

Inferred from the click, scroll and funnel data. Confirm with surveys and session recordings (see section 09).

Mobile-first and fast

The Hairess sessions here are mobile, about 73 seconds long, and mostly spent on the first screen. Decisions happen in the first scroll, so anything below 20% depth is a bonus.

Price-stretched, not price-blind

Pay Later is the most tapped element on the page, and "Starting at ₹499" pages convert 4× better than average. ₹1,799 feels like a considered purchase. Making it feel affordable (₹600 × 3, cost per use against salon blowouts) is the unlock.

Visual verifier

Heavy gallery, zoom and reel engagement. She needs to see the result on hair like hers before she trusts the claim. Copy alone ("ionic tech", "damage-free") doesn't do it.

Delivery- and risk-anxious

Lots of pincode checks, origin questions and footer visits to policies. She wants to know when it arrives, whether she can return it and whether the warranty is real, and the page currently makes her hunt for each.

Proof-seeker who rarely finds it

The visitors who reach reviews spend the most time there of anywhere on the page. Social proof moves her, but it sits at 62% depth.

A first-time category buyer (Hairess)

A hot-air brush is less familiar than a trimmer: "Will it straighten my curly hair?", "Is it like a Dyson?", "Will it fry my hair?" The view-to-cart gap against Fuzzoff points to unanswered how-to and suitability questions.

Working persona: "I want salon-smooth hair without the salon bill, but I've been burned by gadgets that don't work on my hair. Show me it works on someone like me, tell me when it'll arrive, and make the price feel easy."

09 · Expand the data

What to add so the next read is sharper

Today we can see what happens on the page, but not who it happens to or why. These additions close that gap.

GoKwik checkout data: no access yet, and the biggest blind spot. GoKwik runs everything from Checkout to order: phone and OTP, address, payment method (COD, UPI, card, Pay Later via Snapmint), payment failures and drop-offs at each step. None of it reaches GA4 or Shopify in a usable form. That's why we can see that 82% of checkouts fail but can't see where or why. With access to the GoKwik merchant dashboard (step funnel, payment-method split, COD vs prepaid, RTO and drop-off reasons), the checkout half of the journey becomes visible, and the Shopify vs GA4 order gap can be reconciled.

GA4 events to add (custom)

  • emi_open, emi_cta_click
  • review_interact, faq_open (which question)
  • scroll_section_view per PDP section

Clarity setup

  • Custom tags for traffic source, new vs returning, and cart value, so heatmaps can be filtered
  • Smart events: ATC, EMI open, checkout start, purchase. Then compare heatmaps of buyers and non-buyers

Business data to request

  • Shopify orders by sales channel (Online Store vs GoKwik) for the same window, to fix the 0.05% reading
  • GoKwik checkout funnel and the payment-method split
  • Top on-site search terms and zero-result searches

10 · Competitor analysis

Six competitor product pages reviewed in October 2026: Ikonic, AGARO, VEGA, Dyson, Alan Truman and Sirona (the closest rival to Fuzzoff).

Where Bombae already leads

  • Price and warranty. At ₹1,799 with a 2-year warranty, Hairess is the cheapest of the comparable hot-air brushes and has the longest warranty except Dyson. The page doesn't say so at the top, and it contradicts itself (1.5 years at the sticky button).
  • Review volume and UGC. 205 reviews with customer photos beats every mid-price rival. Most show no reviews or no photos. The advantage is wasted at 62% depth.
  • Pay-in-instalments exists. Only Dyson presents EMI well. Bombae is the only brand under ₹3k offering it, but the modal is broken.

Where competitors are ahead

  1. Dyson

    Four reassurance tiles directly under the price

    Cashback and no-cost EMI · COD up to ₹25,000 · 10-day no-questions return · 2-year warranty. Every main objection is answered before the shopper scrolls. Bombae's warranty, returns and delivery information is spread between the bullets, the sticky bar and the footer.

  2. Dyson

    EMI shown as a monthly amount, right under the price

    "₹1,913/month" is shown as part of the price, not behind a button. For Hairess, "or ₹600 × 3, 0% interest" inline with the price makes the same point without a modal.

  3. AGARO

    Final price after the code is shown for you

    The "Calculate Savings" widget shows ₹1,804 after the coupon, so the shopper never has to do the maths or find the code at checkout.

  4. Ikonic · AGARO

    Faster checkout entry

    Ikonic puts a one-tap UPI "Buy Now" above Add to Cart, and AGARO uses Razorpay Magic one-click. With Bombae's checkout completion at about 24%, a direct-to-payment path for the hero product is worth testing.

  5. Sirona · Alan Truman

    Objection-led education

    Sirona has how-to by body area, a 10-question myth-busting FAQ and a cost table against salon waxing and cream. Alan Truman has "common mistakes", curly-hair guidance and wet vs dry use. Bombae's FAQ slot only holds legal fields (Country of Origin / Marketed By / Use Before).

  6. Sirona

    Volume proof next to the title

    "13,486 units sold recently" next to the rating. Hairess sells about 3,200 a month and could say "3,000+ bought this month".

  7. Dyson

    "What's in the box" with each attachment explained

    This justifies the multi-tool claim. It suits Hairess (dryer head + volumiser head) and Fuzzoff 6-in-1 vs Pro 8-in-1.

Open gaps nobody fills

None of the six shows an automatic delivery date, review photos or videos near the top, before/after results, or real hair-type guidance in the mid-price range. If Bombae is first to do these well on mobile, it gets a lead the others haven't claimed.
